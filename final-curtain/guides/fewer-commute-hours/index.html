Where Would Five Fewer Commute Hours Go?

October 30, 2026

A shorter commute becomes easier to picture when the change has a destination. An extra hour on a weekday might hold a family meal, a walk or some breathing room around existing responsibilities.

Before giving those hours away, work out which ones actually change and when they become available.

By IDEASpike, the developer of Final Curtain.

Make the Before-and-After Explicit

Imagine someone who travels one hour in total on each of five working days. Their entered commute is five hours a week. In an alternative arrangement, all five workdays take place at home and the commute becomes zero.

Hold paid work at forty hours in both versions. The difference in work plus commuting is five hours per working week. Over an illustrative 48 working weeks, that is 5 × 48 = 240 hours.

The number describes these two assumptions. A hybrid arrangement with three office days would leave three commuting hours and release two, using the same one-hour round trip. Someone already working from home might have no commute time to release.

Enter the Responsibilities That Move

Check what the commute currently contains. If you collect someone on the way home, that trip may still be needed. If grocery shopping is bundled into the route, a separate journey may appear elsewhere. Work preparation and the workday's start or finish can change too.

Update those entries before calling the entire difference available. A comparison becomes useful when it reflects the arrangement you could actually have.

Avoid counting an activity twice. If the full work-and-travel amount already includes the commute, do not also add a separate five-hour commute entry.

Give the Difference a First Draft

For the full five-hour illustration, one possible allocation is:

Chosen useHours each working week
Family time1.5
Outdoors or movement1.5
Reading or learning1
Flexibility1
Total5

These are example choices, not a recommendation. Keeping some of the difference flexible can make the plan more usable on variable days.

Check the Actual Time Slots

Five released hours may arrive as ten half-hour pieces, split across mornings and evenings. A weekly total alone cannot tell you whether they can accommodate a two-hour class.

Choose an activity that fits the available slots, or identify what would need to move to make a longer block. Include travel for the new activity. A course with a ninety-minute session and a thirty-minute round trip occupies two hours.

The placement check can also reveal a useful choice to leave a small gap open. A calmer transition between work and home can be a deliberate use of time even without another named activity.

Keep the Comparison Small

Write a current week and a possible alternative on the weekly worksheet. Keep work hours and other assumptions constant initially. Change the commute, add responsibilities that move, then allocate only the resulting difference.

If the arrangement becomes real, revisit the inputs. The comparison is a plan to learn from, rather than evidence that the hours have already changed.
