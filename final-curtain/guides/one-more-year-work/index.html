One More Year at Work: Write Down the Time Trade-Off

October 30, 2026

“One more year” can be easier to think about when the working pattern and the alternative are both written down. A time comparison gives one part of that decision a concrete form.

Start with the hours you would actually enter, including the time around work.

By IDEASpike, the developer of Final Curtain.

Compare Two Specific Arrangements

Imagine a fictional current arrangement with forty paid hours and five hours of commuting each working week. An alternative contains twenty paid hours and three hours of commuting.

Entered activityCurrent arrangementAlternative arrangement
Paid work40 hours20 hours
Commuting5 hours3 hours
Total45 hours23 hours

The difference is twenty-two hours per working week. Over an illustrative 48 working weeks, that is 22 × 48 = 1,056 hours allocated differently.

Someone ending paid work entirely, changing roles or keeping the same commute would need another set of inputs. The table is a worked example, not a recommendation or a claim about a typical retirement transition.

Put the Rest of Life Back In

Keep sleep, meals, household work, care, administration and other responsibilities in both versions. Adjust them only when there is a reason the alternative would change them.

Then name possible uses for some of the difference. A weekday visit, a regular course or a quieter routine may help describe what the option means to you. Leave some time flexible rather than assuming every released hour needs a new purpose.

Look at the calendar as well. Workdays, travel and other people's availability can shape whether an activity fits even when the total suggests capacity.

Write Down What the Model Leaves Open

A time comparison does not determine income, pension benefits, healthcare costs, taxes or the resources required for retirement. Those questions need their own assessment.

The model also cannot determine how you will feel about a transition. Work can provide structure, relationships, interest and other things you may value. Include the activities you want to preserve when imagining an alternative week.

Useful assumptions to write beside the table include working weeks, work end dates, recurring responsibilities and uncertainties you need to resolve. A precise total should not hide an uncertain input.

Keep Annual and Lifetime Pictures Distinct

The 48-week calculation above is a simplified annual illustration. A longer projection can include different work end dates, trips and other finite activities. Its result may therefore differ from multiplying the weekly difference by a number of years.

Keep the chosen horizon and cutoff dates visible when comparing longer-term plans. Change one assumption at a time initially so the source of the difference is understandable.

Use the weekly worksheet to start the conversation. It works for full-time, part-time and post-work scenarios without deciding which one you should choose.
